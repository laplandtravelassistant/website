What to wear in Rovaniemi: a winter clothing guide

Travel tips · Updated 21 October 2026

Short answer: Dress in three layers: a wool or synthetic base layer, a warm mid layer and a windproof outer jacket and trousers. Add warm boots with room for your toes, thick wool socks, mittens, a hat that covers your ears and a neck warmer. Skip cotton and jeans. Winter days in Rovaniemi are usually between about −5 °C and −14 °C, and cold snaps can drop much lower.

The three-layer rule

Several thin layers keep you warmer than one thick coat. The air trapped between the layers is what holds the heat, and you can take one off when you step indoors.

1. Base layer

Long-sleeved thermal top and long johns in merino wool or synthetic fabric, worn next to the skin. Wool stays warm even when it's a little damp and doesn't smell after a long day. Avoid cotton: it holds sweat and makes you cold fast.

2. Mid layer

A fleece or wool sweater, plus warm trousers or fleece pants over your long johns. On the coldest days, a thin down or synthetic insulated jacket works well here.

3. Outer layer

A windproof, water-resistant winter jacket and snow trousers. Wind is what makes the cold bite, so blocking it matters more than extra thickness. Pick a jacket long enough to cover your lower back and a hood that fits over your hat.

Hands, feet and head

This is where people get cold first, and where good kit makes the biggest difference.

Local tip: The two things that make the biggest difference are good mittens and wool socks under your winter boots. Fingers are usually the first thing to freeze, so wear thinner gloves under your mittens.

  • Mittens, not just gloves. Mittens keep your fingers together and are much warmer. Wear thin liner gloves underneath so you can take the mittens off for photos without touching anything with bare skin.
  • Warm winter boots with a thick sole and some room inside. Tight boots cut circulation, and cold feet follow. Many people go half a size up to fit thick socks.
  • Wool socks. One good thick pair, or a thin wool pair under a thick one. Pack spares, because damp socks get cold quickly.
  • A hat that covers your ears, ideally wool or fleece-lined.
  • A tube scarf or neck warmer to cover your face and neck. We strongly recommend one. On snowmobiles and in wind, a balaclava is even better.

What to wear for activities

How cold you feel depends on what you're doing. Walking keeps you warm. Sitting still, or moving fast in the wind, doesn't.

  • Northern lights tours: on tours by car, much of the time is spent in a warm car driving to clear skies. Don't get too warm inside, or you'll feel the cold as soon as you step out to watch the lights.
  • Snowmobile safaris: with proper windproof winter clothing and a helmet, snowmobiling isn't as cold as many people expect. Cover your face with a neck warmer or balaclava and wear mittens.
  • Snowshoeing and other active trips: you warm up quickly when you move a lot. Dress in layers you can open or take off, so you don't start to sweat.
  • Husky rides: the dogs kick up snow and you'll sit or stand in the sled. Waterproof outer layers and mittens are key.
  • Reindeer sleigh rides: a calm ride sitting still, which is when cold feet start. Wear your warmest boots and socks.
  • Walking around town: roads and pavements are well maintained, but they can get slippery. Wear boots with a good grip and always watch your step.

Dressing children for the cold

Children get cold faster than adults and don't always say so. A one-piece snowsuit over wool layers works best for small children. Bring spare mittens and socks, and check their hands, feet and cheeks often, especially on activities where they sit still. Head inside for a warm drink before anyone gets really cold.

Common mistakes

  • Too few layers. It's easier to take a layer off than to warm up once you're cold.
  • Cotton next to the skin. Jeans and cotton T-shirts hold moisture and make you cold.
  • Boots that are too tight, or too many socks squeezed into normal boots.
  • Only one pair of gloves. They get damp, so bring a spare pair.
  • Overdressing when you're moving. On active trips like snowshoeing you warm up fast and start to sweat. When you stop, the sweat turns cold and so do you.
  • Keeping everything on indoors. Going into a shopping centre or café? Take some layers off so you don't sweat, or you'll feel very cold when you go back out.
  • Forgetting the dry air. Cold air is very dry. Face cream and lip balm make a big difference.
  • A dead phone. Batteries drain fast in the cold. Keep your phone in an inside pocket and bring a power bank.

Packing checklist

  • Thermal top and long johns (merino or synthetic), 2 sets
  • Fleece or wool sweater
  • Warm trousers or fleece pants
  • Windproof winter jacket with a hood
  • Snow trousers
  • Warm winter boots with good grip
  • Wool socks, several pairs
  • Mittens and thin liner gloves

  • Neck warmer, buff or balaclava
  • Sunglasses (from February)
  • Face cream and lip balm
  • Power bank
  • Ice grips for your shoes (optional)

Questions

What should I wear in Rovaniemi in December?

Full winter layers: a wool or synthetic base layer, a fleece or wool mid layer and a windproof jacket and snow trousers. Add warm boots, wool socks, mittens, a hat and a neck warmer. Average temperatures in December are around −5 to −11 °C.

Are jeans OK in Lapland?

Not on their own. Jeans are cotton, so they hold moisture and give no warmth when it's cold. If you wear them in town, put long johns underneath. For activities, wear warm trousers or snow trousers.

Do I need special boots for Rovaniemi?

You need warm, insulated winter boots with a thick sole and good grip, with room for thick socks. Normal trainers or fashion boots will leave you with cold feet within minutes outside.

Gloves or mittens?

Mittens are warmer. Thin liner gloves underneath let you use your phone or camera without bare hands.
